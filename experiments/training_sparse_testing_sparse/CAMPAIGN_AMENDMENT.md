All four preferred profiles are complete. CIFAR-10 ViT batch 256 was rejected because the grayscale compact 40% upper-support step left only 0.809% estimated memory headroom, below the required 15%; batch 128 passed all 19 conditions. No accuracy result was used for this choice.

A profile uses complete real training data for its input/support selection, all active conditions, fresh optimizer steps, and upper-support capacity panels. Each executed candidate/condition requests two warmup and three measured steps, with optimizer-state allocation included in the capacity peak. The profile LR is fixed at 0.0003 and is distinct from the final training LR rule below. The selection rule requires finite loss/gradients/parameters, all conditions passing, and at least 15% estimated memory headroom. Among eligible candidates it chooses the largest batch within 5% of the best harmonic-mean condition throughput. These short measurements support a resource choice; they do not establish convergence, accuracy, an accuracy-optimal batch, or isolated inference speedup. Larger candidates skipped after a capacity limit remain explicitly untested.

The controller verifies profile coverage, worker receipts, scheduler isolation records, and source/input hashes before accepting a group. `outputs/batch_profile/selection.json` records accepted groups and recipes. `experiment_manifest.json` stores those recipes in `recipe_by_group`, keyed by dataset/architecture. A verified group can start its full raw pilot while another group remains pending; an unselected group cannot launch using the inherited placeholder. The pilot's prespecified clean-validation gate must pass before its main conditions are released. For this launch every group was already verified. The manifest base batch is null and all four `recipe_by_group` entries are required; there is no fallback to batch 8.

For each accepted group, physical training batch, effective batch, and validation/test/corruption batch all equal the selected batch. Every active condition and seed in that group uses the same recipe. Training is float32 without gradient accumulation; both loaders use zero workers. Clean data are memory-resident, and augmentation and sample order remain keyed by seed, epoch, and sample identity.

AdamW uses base LR `3e-4 * sqrt(B / 128)`, betas `(0.9, 0.999)`, weight decay 0.05, gradient clipping 1, and cosine decay to 1e-6. MNIST retains 50 epochs and 5 warmup epochs; CIFAR-10 retains 200 and 10. Dropout remains zero and stochastic depth 0.1. This gives LR 0.000424264 for batch 256, 0.000848528 for batch 1024, 0.0006 for batch 512, and 0.0003 for batch 128. ViT jobs use the 48/94 GB partitions; Swin jobs can use the available 11 GB class and larger GPUs. Actual hardware remains recorded per attempt; placement does not change the scientific group recipe.

Validated raw datasets remain shared at their original absolute locations. The four split files and four active normalization fits are byte-identical local copies, with origins and hashes in `provenance/reused_inputs.json`. They remain valid because optimizer batch size does not change the frontend or fitted population. The dedicated environment and its pins are unchanged.

Official inference latency still uses batches 1 and 8, independent of the training batch amendment. It requires all main evaluations and the separate isolated measurement protocol. Accuracy, timing, and runtime tables must identify this campaign and its group recipes and must not mix in the archived batch-8 results.
